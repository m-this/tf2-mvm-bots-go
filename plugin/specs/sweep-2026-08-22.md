# Every map, one night

Seven maps, six waves each, nobody on the server. Then the bugs it found, then
the same three maps again to see whether fixing them moved anything.

What follows is the numbers and what they meant. Where a fix is claimed, the
before and after are both here; where something is still wrong, it says so and
says what the next step is rather than guessing at a cause.

Run with `testbed/sweep.sh`, read with `go run ./testbed/sweepreport`.
Results in `testbed/results/sweep-fixed` and `testbed/results/sweep-verify`.

## The short version

- **No crashes.** Forty waves in the first sweep, seventeen in the second, zero
  segfaults and zero watchdog kills.
- **Frames are fine.** Mean frame time 15.00–15.27 ms against a 15 ms tick
  budget, and the worst single frame of the night was 194 ms. The watchdog
  fires around a second.
- **Three separate bugs were stopping engineers from finishing a nest**, and
  all three were invisible from a start-of-wave snapshot. Finding them needed
  sampling through the wave.
- **The weakest class is the Demoman**, at 1634 damage a wave across all forty,
  against the Heavy's 8594.
- **The bots lose more than they win**: 14 waves cleared, 26 lost.

## What was wrong, and what it was

### The dispenser build ended itself three seconds in

`m_ctSentrySafe` is "the sentry is finished and in no trouble" with three
seconds of life on it, refreshed by the idle action and only by the idle
action. Suspending the idle action stops its update running, so the flag
expires three seconds after the dispenser build starts, whatever the sentry is
actually doing. The build read the flag and ended itself with "Sentry not
safe", every time.

It finished a dispenser only where the walk and the placement both fitted
inside those three seconds. That is the whole of the Mannworks column: six
waves out of six begun with no dispenser, 13% uptime, and since a teleporter
needs a dispenser first, no teleporter either.

`IsSentrySafe` asks the sentry now. Mannworks went from 13% to 39% dispenser
uptime and 24 defender deaths a wave instead of 32.

### An engineer with no sentry considered moving instead of building

The idle action asks whether the nest should advance before it does anything
else and returns without acting when the answer is yes, because advancing is
what it is about to do. An engineer whose sentry had just been destroyed had
nothing to move and the answer was yes anyway.

So he never rebuilt. On Bigrock that was most of a wave: no sentry, no
dispenser, standing in the middle of the map, with every branch that would have
rebuilt one sitting behind that return.

It was expensive as well as wrong. Each of those frames ran `PickBuildArea`
twice, and `PickBuildArea` calls `GetBombInfo`, which walks every nav area on
the map. Sixty-six times a second, per engineer, for as long as he had no
sentry. That is the watchdog crash TODO item 12 could not pin down.

Bigrock sentry uptime went from 64% to 78% and its no-dispenser waves from one
to none.

### The sentry was the last building still aiming at its own feet

A building goes down in front of the man and never under him. The dispenser and
the teleporter both knew that; the sentry walked onto the nest point, stood on
it, and aimed at the ground it was standing on. Between rounds it mostly worked
because the engineer is teleported onto the point. In the middle of a wave,
having walked there, it did not.

There was no clock on any of it either, so an engineer who could not place one
stayed in that action for the rest of the wave.

## The classes

Forty waves. Damage per wave is read against the waves that class actually held
a seat in, not against every wave in the sweep.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| heavy | 8594 | 566 | 28 | 31.5% |
| spy | 7818 | 16 | 6 | 6.1% |
| engineer | 4736 | 701 | 40 | 24.8% |
| pyro | 3840 | 214 | 16 | 8.0% |
| scout | 3102 | 292 | 34 | 13.8% |
| soldier | 2232 | 182 | 24 | 7.0% |
| **demoman** | **1634** | 212 | 40 | 8.6% |
| medic | 45 | 7 | 28 | 0.2% |

**The Demoman is the weakest seat in the lineup and it is in every lineup.** It
plays every one of the forty waves and produces less than a fifth of what the
Heavy does with the same time.

It is not aim. 1634 damage for 212 kills is 308 damage a kill; the Pyro spends
354 for its kills and the Heavy 425. The Demoman is efficient with what it
fires and does not fire enough. Three things are worth checking before touching
the aim code, in this order:

1. `ShouldUseStickyLauncher` only lets the launcher out for a giant, the bomb
   carrier, or a crowd of three within 1200 units. Everything else is the Iron
   Bomber's four-round clip.
2. Time spent laying a trap is time not shooting, and nothing counts it.
3. The tank case is already handled, so it is not that: the launcher scores 110
   against a tank, above the primary.

Two other readings from the same table:

- **The Spy's 7818 a wave is a measurement of six waves on one map** and its 16
  kills against that damage say most of it was chip damage, not backstabs. It
  appeared because Ghost Town had no lineup and drew one. Do not read it as a
  case for seating a Spy.
- **No lineup on any installed map seats a Sniper or a Spy**, and every one of
  the seven carries hand-walked sniper spots. Six spots a map, authored by
  somebody who flew the map, and nothing has ever stood on them.

What kills the defenders, whole sweep: robot Soldiers 170, Demomen 137,
Pyros 113, Scouts 59, Heavies 54, Snipers 29. Blast damage is what the team has
no answer to.

## Ghost Town

Ghost Town was the only installed map with no `Composition`, so the lineup mode
named its team. It drew two engineers, a spy, a soldier and a demoman: no
scout, so nobody was collecting the credits, and no medic. It lost all six
waves while both engineers held level three nests with a dispenser standing 96%
of the time.

It now names the same six as Coaltown. That made the team sane and **did not
change the result**: still nought for six, and the engineer columns are now
perfect. So the lineup was not what was losing Ghost Town.

What it has that no other installed map has is no `EngineerNest` data at all,
so `PickBuildArea` is guessing where to hold. Its `SniperSpot` block is
byte-identical to Coaltown's, which suggests somebody believed the two share a
layout. If that is right, Coaltown's nest, dispenser and teleporter-exit data
belongs in it and Coaltown clears four of six. I could not verify the two
coordinate spaces are the same from outside the game, and copying coordinates
on a hunch is how bad map data gets written, so this wants somebody in the map
with `sm_dump_spot`.

## Still open, worst first

1. **Ghost Town loses every wave.** Not the lineup. Most likely no authored
   nests. Needs a walk.
2. **Mannworks still begins four waves in six without a dispenser**, at 39%
   uptime rather than 13%. Better, not fixed.
3. **The Demoman.** Weakest seat, in every lineup, and the three checks above
   are the order to do them in.
4. **Bigrock holds a level three sentry for 22% of a wave.** Sentry uptime is
   78%, so he has one; he cannot keep it upgraded. Metal, most likely, which
   points back at the dispenser.
5. **Mannhattan's three authored dispenser spots are all unusable**, each of
   them the better part of a storey below the nest nearest to it. The fallback
   puts the dispenser 95 units from the sentry, which is better than the
   authored spots would have, so nothing is broken and somebody's walking is
   being thrown away. `testbed/checkspots.py` prints this per map.
6. **The sniper spots on all seven maps have never been used**, because no
   lineup seats a Sniper.
